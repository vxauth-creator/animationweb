The most common pattern I see in agency work is this: motion bolted on after the build, 3D dropped in to tick a box, "premium feel" expressed entirely through gradients. The code ships, the marketing site looks great in screenshots, and the actual user experience is heavy, slow, and a little embarrassing on a mid-tier phone.

We try to do the opposite.

## Motion is a load-bearing element

Every animation in our stack has to answer one question: **what does this communicate?** A magnetic hover communicates *interactive*. A staggered reveal communicates *structure*. A camera drift in a hero scene communicates *space and depth*. If a motion doesn't answer that question, it gets cut.

The corollary is that a *good* motion system disappears. Users don't think "oh, nice animation". They think "this feels right". That's the bar.

## 3D should respect the GPU it runs on

Three lessons we've learned the hard way:

1. **Adaptive rendering tier.** A blanket 60-fps target across all devices is naive. Detect tier on mount (`navigator.hardwareConcurrency`, `deviceMemory`, `prefers-reduced-motion`, viewport width) and ship different complexity per tier.
2. **Bloom is a tax.** Post-processing doubles your render passes. Use it on *high* tier only, and downgrade at runtime if you measure sustained FPS drops.
3. **Suspend, don't block.** Heavy assets (HDRIs, large models) belong in `<Suspense>`, never on the critical path.

## The discipline tradeoff

A premium feel is not free. It costs you milliseconds of build time, kilobytes of bundle, and weeks of taste-driven iteration. The way to keep it from costing you *credibility* is to put performance budgets in writing on day one.

For our standard build:
- Lighthouse Performance ≥ 95 on mid-tier mobile
- LCP ≤ 1.8s on a Moto G4 emulation
- TBT ≤ 200ms
- CLS ≤ 0.05

If a feature breaks one of these, it doesn't ship until we either find the speed or remove the feature. There's no third option.

## Why premium feels expensive

Premium feels expensive because you can *tell* the team didn't compromise. Every interaction is intentional. Every load state is graceful. The dark mode isn't a `darkMode: true` toggle — it's a deliberate palette tuned for OLED. The 404 page isn't a placeholder — it's a moment.

That's what we're building toward.
